# Whiteboard 1 of 3 — Indian Education Governance, Authorities, Powers & Standards

We will plan three interconnected whiteboards, but we will not create any visual board yet. First, we will define the complete content architecture, including every major section, parent node, child node, leaf node, relationship, and information field.

The first whiteboard will answer one central question:

Who has the legal authority to make each decision in India's education system, from pre-primary education through universities, professional education, examinations, qualifications and government recruitment?

It will be designed as a navigable hierarchy, not a collection of text-heavy boxes. Each parent node will branch into child nodes, and each important authority will have a detailed information card. Relationships between bodies will be labelled precisely so that legal authority, administrative reporting, coordination and examination operations are never confused.

## The three-board architecture

Whiteboard 1 — Governance and authority

Constitution → legislatures → governments → ministries → regulators → boards → examination agencies → institutions, with powers, laws and limits.

We are specifying this board now

Whiteboard 2 — Institutions and qualifications

IITs, NITs, IIMs, ISB, AIIMS, universities, school boards, professional institutes, their programmes, qualifications, responsibilities and recognition.

Whiteboard 3 — Examinations, timelines and career routes

Class 10/12 → undergraduate → postgraduate → professional qualifications → research, defence, civil services, banking and other careers.

## 1. What Whiteboard 1 must contain

The first board must be the source of truth for authority and responsibility. When Whiteboard 2 says an institution offers a BTech, MBBS, MBA or LLB, Whiteboard 1 should explain which authorities govern the applicable standards. When Whiteboard 3 says an examination leads to an admission opportunity, Whiteboard 1 should identify who is empowered to conduct the exam, set its rules and administer the relevant process.

### Scope boundaries

- Geographic scope: India, including Union, State and Union Territory arrangements.
- Education scope: early childhood, school education, higher education, technical education, professional education, vocational education and research.
- Examination scope: school-board examinations, entrance and eligibility examinations, university examinations and government recruitment examinations.
- Governance scope: constitutional powers, legislation, executive policy, regulatory standards, institutional administration, oversight and appeals.
- Detail level: parent → domain → subdomain → authority → function → power → procedure → limitation → evidence/source.
- Accuracy rule: no universal chain of command will be drawn where the real relationship is independent, statutory, advisory or coordinative.

The three boards will share identifiers for bodies and examinations. That will let a user follow a node from governance to an institution and then to the examination or career route it affects.

# 3. Section 00 — Legend, scope and navigation

This section should sit in the top-left corner of the board. It establishes how to interpret the entire diagram.

## 00.1 Board identity

- Title: Indian Education Governance, Authorities, Powers & Standards
- Subtitle: Constitutional, legislative, executive, regulatory, examination and oversight architecture
- Coverage: India — Union, States and Union Territories
- Purpose: Identify the competent authority for every education-related decision.
- Companion boards: Board 2 — Institutions & Qualifications; Board 3 — Examinations, Timelines & Careers.

## 00.2 Node types

| Node type                 | What it represents                                                          | Example                        |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Constitutional source     | A constitutional provision or principle                                     | Article 21A                    |
| Legislature               | A law-making body                                                           | Parliament                     |
| Executive authority       | A government exercising executive functions                                 | Ministry of Education          |
| Administrative department | A department managing government work                                       | Department of Higher Education |
| Statutory regulator       | A body established or empowered by legislation                              | UGC                            |
| Examination authority     | A body conducting designated tests                                          | NTA                            |
| Academic board            | A body responsible for specified academic or examination functions          | CBSE                           |
| Institution               | A university, college or school                                             | IIT or central university      |
| Oversight body            | A body auditing, reviewing or investigating matters within its jurisdiction | CAG                            |
| Process                   | A sequence of legally or administratively defined steps                     | Counselling                    |

## 00.3 Relationship types

This is particularly important. A single arrow style must never be used to represent every relationship.

| Relationship label          | Meaning                                                              | Example                                                     |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| `CONSTITUTIONALLY EMPOWERS` | Legal competence comes from the Constitution                         | Constitution → Parliament                                   |
| `LEGISLATES`                | Makes legislation within constitutional competence                   | Parliament → central education Act                          |
| `ADMINISTRATIVELY OVERSEES` | Exercises administrative oversight under the applicable framework    | Ministry → department                                       |
| `ESTABLISHED BY LAW`        | A body exists or derives powers under a particular Act               | Act → statutory regulator                                   |
| `SETS STANDARDS`            | Prescribes standards within its remit                                | Regulator → regulated programme                             |
| `CONDUCTS EXAM`             | Operates an assigned examination                                     | NTA → examination                                           |
| `ADMINISTERS ADMISSION`     | Conducts counselling or admission allocation                         | Designated counselling authority → seat allocation          |
| `AWARDS QUALIFICATION`      | Awards a degree or qualification under its legal framework           | University → degree                                         |
| `RECOGNISES / APPROVES`     | Exercises a specified recognition or approval power                  | Applicable regulator → programme                            |
| `ACCREDITS`                 | Assesses quality under an accreditation framework                    | NBA → programme                                             |
| `RANKS`                     | Produces rankings using a defined methodology                        | NIRF → ranking                                              |
| `FUNDS`                     | Provides or administers funding                                      | Government → scheme/institution                             |
| `COORDINATES`               | Facilitates coordination without necessarily commanding another body | Advisory or coordinating body → participating organisations |
| `ADVISES`                   | Provides recommendations rather than binding directions by default   | Advisory body → government                                  |
| `AUDITS`                    | Examines accounts or expenditure within jurisdiction                 | CAG → covered public body                                   |

| `JUDICIALLY REVIEWS`        | Courts examine legality or constitutionality                         | Court → government/regulatory decision                      |

A body can have multiple outgoing relationships. For example, a regulator may set standards, recognise qualifications, inspect institutions and take enforcement action, but only where the relevant legislation permits each activity.

## 00.4 Authority status labels

Each body must carry a classification:

- Constitutional body or authority.
- Union ministry or government department.
- State or UT government department.
- Statutory regulator.
- Statutory board or commission.
- Autonomous organisation.
- Society or registered organisation.
- University or educational institution.
- Advisory body.
- Accreditation body.
- Ranking framework or programme.
- Examination agency.
- Oversight or grievance body.

These categories must not be treated as interchangeable. An autonomous organisation is not necessarily a statutory regulator, and a ranking framework is not an approval authority.

# 4. Section 01 — Constitutional foundation

This section forms the legal root of the entire board. The Constitution should be the top parent node, with individual articles and their implications as child nodes.

## 01.1 Constitution of India

Parent node: Constitution of India

Child nodes:

- Fundamental rights.
- Directive Principles of State Policy.
- Distribution of legislative powers.
- Union and State executive powers.
- Parliamentary and State legislative processes.
- Conflict resolution between Union and State legislation.
- Constitutional amendment.
- Judicial review.

### Leaf card: Article 21A

- Subject: Right to education.
- Legal meaning: The State must provide free and compulsory education to children aged 6–14 in the manner determined by law.
- Implementation link: Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009.
- Affected domain: Elementary education.
- What it enables: A constitutional basis for the State's obligations concerning education for the specified age group.
- What it does not mean: It does not automatically make every educational service at every level free, nor does it prescribe every school examination.
- Cross-board links: School governance; RTE compliance; school access and progression.

### Leaf card: Article 45

- Subject: Early childhood care and education.
- Legal character: Directive Principle of State Policy.
- Affected domain: Early childhood development and education below six years.
- Implementation links: Early childhood programmes, preschool systems and relevant Union/State policies.
- Important limitation: It is not the same legal mechanism as the enforceable fundamental right under Article 21A.

### Leaf card: Article 46

- Subject: Promotion of educational and economic interests of weaker sections, especially Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes.
- Legal character: Directive Principle.
- Affected domain: Educational access, welfare and inclusion.
- Potential implementation instruments: Scholarships, targeted schemes and other lawful support programmes.
- Limitation: A Directive Principle does not, by itself, prescribe a particular entrance exam or individual scholarship entitlement.

### Leaf card: Article 246 and the Seventh Schedule

This card must branch into three lists:

1. Union List.
2. State List.
3. Concurrent List.

The important education-specific child nodes are:

- Concurrent List, Entry 25: Education, including technical and medical education and universities, subject to the specified Union List entries.
- Union List, Entry 66: Coordination and determination of standards in higher education or research and scientific and technical institutions.
- Other applicable Union List entries: These may matter where a particular institution, subject or national framework falls under a separate constitutional provision.

The card's main explanation: Parliament and State Legislatures both have legislative roles in education, but their powers are not identical in every domain. The applicable entry and governing legislation must be identified before deciding who can change a rule.

### Leaf card: Article 254

- Subject: Inconsistency between Union and State laws on Concurrent List matters.
- Main rule: Where the constitutional conditions are met, a conflicting parliamentary law generally prevails over a State law.
- Exception: Article 254(2) provides a route for certain State laws receiving Presidential assent, subject to the Constitution and Parliament's continuing power to legislate.
- Use on the board: Connects Union law, State law and conflict-resolution mechanisms.
- Important limitation: Do not label every difference between central and State education policies as a legal conflict. The actual statutes and provisions must be compared.

### Leaf card: Article 368

- Subject: Constitutional amendment.
- Authority: Parliament, using the constitutionally prescribed procedure.
- Additional requirement: Ratification by at least half of the State Legislatures for specified categories of amendments.
- What it means for education: A structural constitutional change cannot be implemented by an Education Minister merely issuing a circular.

### Additional constitutional nodes

Include:

- Article 14 — equality before the law and equal protection.
- Article 15 — non-discrimination and constitutionally permitted special provisions.
- Article 29(2) — specified non-discrimination protection in admission to State-maintained or State-aided educational institutions.
- Article 30 — rights of religious and linguistic minorities to establish and administer educational institutions, subject to the applicable constitutional framework.
- Article 350A — facilities for instruction in the mother tongue at the primary stage for children belonging to linguistic minority groups, as specified.
- Article 350B — Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities.
- Article 32 and Article 226 — constitutional remedies and judicial review through the Supreme Court and High Courts, respectively, subject to their jurisdiction.
- Article 12 — definition of “State” for the relevant fundamental-rights framework.
- Article 73 and Article 162 — Union and State executive-power frameworks, subject to constitutional limits.

Board design requirement: Each constitutional node should link to the laws and bodies implementing it. The same provision may connect to multiple branches rather than forcing it into one administrative hierarchy.

# 5. Section 02 — Parliament and central legislation

## Parent: Parliament of India

### Child 02.1 — Lok Sabha

Information to display:

- Role in central legislation.
- Participation in budget and expenditure processes.
- Questions, debates and scrutiny of government policies.
- Role in examining proposed changes to education laws.
- Limitation: A member of Parliament cannot personally change a statutory requirement by sending an informal instruction.

### Child 02.2 — Rajya Sabha

Information to display:

- Role in ordinary legislation.
- Review and debate of proposed laws.
- Participation in constitutional amendments under the applicable process.
- Role in scrutiny of government action.
- Limitation: Its powers and procedures differ from the Lok Sabha in certain financial and legislative matters.

### Child 02.3 — President

Information to display:

- Constitutional role in the legislative process.
- Assent to Bills under the Constitution.
- Ordinance-related powers under the applicable constitutional provisions.
- Formal executive functions and applicable constitutional procedures.
- Limitation: The President is not the day-to-day administrator of all education regulators.

Key distinction: A ministry can propose a scheme, but spending must follow the applicable financial authorisation, budgetary controls and scheme rules.

# 6. Section 03 — Union executive and Ministry of Education

This branch explains how the Union Government administers education policy after the legal authority has been established.

## 03.1 President → 03.2 Prime Minister → 03.3 Union Council of Ministers

These nodes should be connected by constitutional and executive relationships, not shown as though the President personally supervises each examination body.

### Prime Minister node

- Leads the Union Government.
- Coordinates government policy and Cabinet-level priorities through the constitutional executive system.
- Can drive major policy proposals and inter-ministerial coordination through the government machinery.
- Does not independently amend an education Act or override every regulator's statutory functions.

### Union Council of Ministers node

- Advises and supports the Prime Minister in governing.
- Takes or recommends decisions through applicable Cabinet and government processes.
- Coordinates proposals involving multiple ministries.
- Exercises authority according to the Constitution and applicable business rules.

### Union Minister of Education node

Responsibilities:

- Political leadership of the Ministry of Education.
- Policy direction and departmental priorities.
- Oversight of assigned programmes and administrative work.
- Representation in Parliament and government decision-making.
- Proposals for legislation, funding, regulatory reform and national programmes.

Powers, where authorised:

- Approve departmental matters delegated to the minister.
- Direct administrative action through the authorised hierarchy.
- Submit proposals for Cabinet or parliamentary consideration.
- Oversee the implementation of approved policies and schemes.
- Exercise specified powers assigned under laws, rules or government orders.

Limitations:

- Cannot personally enact a parliamentary statute.
- Cannot disregard the Constitution.
- Cannot automatically replace a regulator's statutory decision with a personal instruction.
- Cannot direct every State school as though it were a Union department.
- Cannot guarantee an admission, job, degree or professional licence to an individual.

### Ministers of State node

Include:

- Responsibilities allocated by the government or senior minister.
- Oversight of designated portfolios or programmes.
- Parliamentary and departmental work.
- No automatic independent power to overrule the Cabinet, statutory law or the competent authority.

### Ministry of Education node

Split into exactly two major department children:

1. Department of School Education & Literacy.
2. Department of Higher Education.

For the ministry itself, record:

- Official name.
- Legal/administrative status.
- Ministerial leadership.
- Secretary-level administrative leadership.
- Main functions.
- Relevant budget and schemes.
- Bodies associated with each department.
- Limits of direct control.
- Official organisation chart and work-allocation source.

### Department of School Education & Literacy

Child nodes:

- School policy and programmes.
- Literacy and adult education.
- Elementary education and RTE implementation.
- Teacher-related programmes and educational support.
- National school-related institutions and networks.
- Centrally sponsored schemes.

- Monitoring and programme evaluation.

- University-related matters.
- Technical education coordination.
- Higher-education development and funding.
- Scholarships and research-related schemes.

# 17. Section 14 — How an education decision becomes legally effective

This is the reform and implementation engine of Whiteboard 1. It connects an identified problem to the authority that can lawfully implement a change.

## Parent: Proposed education reform

### Node 14.1 — Identify the problem

Required fields:

- What is wrong?
- Which education level is affected?
- Which examination, course, institution or qualification is affected?
- Is the problem academic, administrative, financial, regulatory or constitutional?
- What existing rule produces the problem?
- Which population is affected?
- What outcome is desired?

### Node 14.2 — Identify the competent authority

The process must answer:

- Does the issue require a constitutional amendment?
- Does it require a new or amended Act?
- Does it require a regulation or statutory rule?
- Can the government change it through an executive policy or scheme?
- Is the decision delegated to a board, university, regulator or examination agency?
- Is it a State matter, Union matter or Concurrent List matter?
- Does the decision require another authority's consultation, approval or concurrence?

### Node 14.3 — Select the legal instrument

| Instrument                      | Who can use it?                                                | What it can accomplish                                                           |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Constitutional amendment        | Parliament under Article 368, with ratification where required | Changes the constitutional text through the prescribed procedure                 |
| Act of Parliament               | Parliament within its legislative competence                   | Creates, changes or repeals statutory requirements                               |
| State Act                       | Competent State Legislature                                    | Creates or changes State law within constitutional competence                    |
| Rules                           | Government or authority empowered by the parent law            | Implements statutory requirements within delegated authority                     |
| Regulations                     | Statutory regulator or other authorised body                   | Prescribes requirements within its delegated statutory powers                    |
| Executive policy                | Competent government authority                                 | Establishes policy direction within the law                                      |
| Government scheme               | Competent government authority, with required approvals        | Creates an administrative programme and its operating framework                  |
| Notification                    | Authority empowered to issue the relevant notification         | Activates or communicates a legal or administrative measure where permitted      |

Not every proposal needs every approval. The board should display these as conditional requirements determined by the governing law, not as a universal checklist that every reform must follow.

### Mandatory notes attached to this matrix

1. The matrix is a high-level summary, not a substitute for the governing statute.
2. An institution can have different powers depending on whether it is a central university, State university, deemed-to-be university, private university, Institute of National Importance or affiliated college.
3. A regulator's power may differ by profession, qualification, institution type and applicable court decisions.
4. A testing agency's authority to conduct an examination does not automatically give it the power to determine admission policy.
5. The same organisation may occupy more than one role in a particular programme. The board must show the specific role attached to the relationship, not assign a single permanent role to every activity.

### Source-register fields

Each node that makes a factual or legal claim should have a source record containing:

- Official source name.
- Official URL.
- Document title.
- Relevant section, provision, notification or paragraph.
- Publication date, if available.
- Effective date or commencement date.
- Last-verified date.
- Verification status.
- Whether the source is primary legislation, official regulation, official government guidance or secondary explanation.
- Notes about amendments, transition or supersession.

### Source priority

1. Constitution of India.
2. Official Act and amendments on India Code or the relevant official legislative source.
3. Official Gazette notifications and commencement notifications.
4. Official regulator rules and regulations.
5. Official government orders and scheme guidelines.
6. Official examination bulletins and admission brochures.
7. Official institution statutes and academic regulations.
8. Official annual reports and organisational charts.
9. Secondary explanatory material only when needed to interpret or contextualise the primary sources.

A claim should not be marked “verified” merely because it appears on an institution's general information page. Powers, recognition, commencement dates and eligibility requirements should be traced to the relevant official source.

## 25. Final specification for Whiteboard 1

The first whiteboard will contain 16 major sections, covering the constitutional foundation, Parliament, Union executive, other ministries, State/UT governments, local administration, school education, higher education, professional regulators, examination authorities, accreditation, funding, oversight, reform implementation, decision rights and source traceability.
