# Whiteboard 2 of 3 — Indian Educational Institutions, Courses, Qualifications & Career Value

Whiteboard 1 establishes who governs education, who sets standards and who has the legal power to make decisions.

Whiteboard 2 will establish what institutions exist, what each institution provides, which programmes it offers, what qualifications students receive, and what those qualifications enable them to pursue next.

We will follow the same approach as Whiteboard 1: first define the complete content architecture, then specify every parent, child and leaf node, including the information fields, relationships, legal recognition and cross-board connections. We will not create the actual whiteboard yet.

The planned destination is an interactive, zoomable whiteboard application, using the previously selected Google Antigravity workflow with React, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind CSS, React Flow, ELK.js and Lucide icons. This answer defines the content that the application must contain; it does not create the application or visual board.

## The three-board system

Whiteboard 1 — Governance

Constitution, ministries, regulators, examination authorities, legal powers and standards.

Whiteboard 2 — Institutions and qualifications

Schools, boards, universities, IITs, IIMs, ISB, AIIMS, professional institutes, courses, degrees and their value.

We are specifying this board now

Whiteboard 3 — Examinations and career pathways

Eligibility, entrance exams, examination stages, calendars, counselling, government recruitment and progression to careers.

## 1. The central purpose of Whiteboard 2

This board must answer ten questions about every important institution and qualification:

1. What is the institution, and what type of organisation is it?
2. Who established it, and under which Act, statute or legal arrangement?
3. Who governs it, and which authority regulates its relevant programmes?
4. What levels of education does it provide?
5. Which specific courses and specialisations does it offer?
6. What is the admission route and the typical duration?
7. What qualification does the student receive, and who awards it?
8. What recognition, accreditation or professional registration applies?
9. What can the student pursue after completing the programme?
10. How does the institution or qualification connect to the governance and examination boards?

The key design principle is that an institution, a course, a qualification, an accreditation and a professional licence are five different things. They must have separate nodes.

For example, an IIT is an institution; BTech is a programme/qualification category; JEE Advanced is an admission examination; an IIT's applicable establishing legislation provides its legal framework; and a particular degree's recognition and professional implications are separate matters.

# 3. Section 00 — Board identity, legend and navigation

This section establishes how the content is classified and searched.

### 00.1 Board identity

- Title: Indian Educational Institutions, Courses & Qualifications.
- Subtitle: Institutions, programme structures, awarding authority, recognition and progression.
- Geography: India, with selected international qualifications accepted or used in India.
- Purpose: Understand what an institution provides, what a qualification means, and what it enables.
- Companion boards: Governance and Authorities; Examinations and Career Pathways.

### 00.2 Entity types

| Entity type         | Example                          | Why it needs a separate node                                                        |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Institution         | IIT Bombay                       | Provides programmes and educational services                                        |
| Institution network | IIT system                       | Groups multiple separately identifiable institutions                                |
| University          | University of Mumbai             | Has a legally defined academic and degree-awarding framework                        |
| School board        | CBSE                             | Conducts specified school examinations and awards school qualifications             |
| Programme           | BTech Computer Engineering       | Defines a particular course of study                                                |
| Qualification       | BTech degree                     | Represents the credential obtained on completion                                    |
| Professional body   | ICAI                             | Administers a professional qualification framework                                  |
| Regulator           | UGC                              | Exercises specified statutory functions                                             |
| Examination         | JEE Advanced                     | Provides a selection result, not a degree                                           |
| Accreditation body  | NBA                              | Assesses programme quality under its framework                                      |
| Ranking framework   | NIRF                             | Compares institutions using a defined methodology                                   |
| Career outcome      | Chartered Accountant or engineer | Describes a possible professional destination, not an automatic result of admission |

### 00.3 Institution classifications

Every institution should carry multiple classification fields rather than just one label:

- Public / private.
- Central / State / other legal category.
- University / college / autonomous institution / school / academy / training provider.
- Degree-awarding / affiliated / examination-conducting / training-only.
- Research-intensive / teaching-focused / professional / vocational.
- Physical / online / distance / blended.
- General education / professional education / defence training.
- Regulated / recognised / approved / accredited, as applicable.

An institution may be public and an Institute of National Importance, for example. These are different classification dimensions and should not be forced into mutually exclusive categories.

### 00.4 Relationship legend

The visual board must distinguish:

- `OFFERS PROGRAMME`
- `AWARDS QUALIFICATION`
- `AFFILIATED TO`
- `ESTABLISHED UNDER`
- `REGULATED BY`
- `APPROVED FOR`
- `ACCREDITED BY`
- `RANKED BY`
- `ADMITS THROUGH`
- `REQUIRES QUALIFICATION`
- `ELIGIBLE FOR FURTHER STUDY`
- `REQUIRES PROFESSIONAL REGISTRATION`
- `POTENTIAL CAREER PATHWAY`

These are separate edges. For example, `RANKED BY` must never be used to imply `RECOGNISED BY`, and `ADMITS THROUGH` must never imply `AWARDS QUALIFICATION`.

Each education-level card should specify:

- Typical entry qualification.
- Typical age or prior study stage, where meaningful.

- Typical duration or range.

### 02.3 What this section must not do

- Treat NCERT as a school board.
- Treat CBSE as the owner of every CBSE-affiliated school.
- Treat KVS and NVS as examination boards.
- Assume that all State boards have identical syllabi or procedures.
- Assume that a qualification is accepted for every further-study programme without checking eligibility.

The IIT network should be a parent node with individual IITs as children.

# 20. Section 17 — Recognition, accreditation and qualification validity

This section prevents the most serious classification errors on the whiteboard.

## 17.1 The five distinct concepts

Legal establishment

Was the institution established through the applicable legal mechanism, and what powers does that mechanism grant?

Degree-awarding authority

Who is legally authorised to award the particular degree or qualification?

Regulatory approval / recognition

Does the institution or programme satisfy the applicable statutory requirements?

Accreditation

How has an institution or programme performed under a specified quality-assessment framework?

Ranking

Where does an institution stand under a particular ranking methodology and reporting year?

These five concepts should have distinct visual nodes and relationship types.

Who provides the education?

Programme

What does the student study?

Qualification

What award does the student receive?

Who recognises it, and what additional requirements apply?

Which further studies, professional pathways or careers may follow?
