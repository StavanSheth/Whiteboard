
# Indian Democracy and Government — Complete Hierarchy

India-wide framework • Maharashtra examples • Position, full form, tenure, authority, population, geographical coverage and administrative reporting • Reference date: 15 October 2026

The central idea is this: India does not have one single hierarchy of people exercising power. It has a constitutional hierarchy, an elected political system, an administrative system, a judiciary and local self-government. They overlap geographically but have different legal powers. The Constitution divides legislative authority between the Union and the States, while local governments receive functions under constitutional provisions and state laws.&#x20;

## Part 1 — The complete hierarchy, from President to citizen

## THE PEOPLE OF INDIA

Citizens • Voters • Constitutional source of democratic legitimacy

Elect, participate, question, petition and seek legal remedies

### A. Union / Central Government

President of India → Prime Minister and Union Council of Ministers → Union ministries and departments → national and regional offices of Union agencies.

Parliament comprises the President, Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha. The President is the constitutional head; the Prime Minister leads the government.

### B. State Government

Governor → Chief Minister and State Council of Ministers → state ministries and departments → divisional, district and field administration.

The Governor is appointed; the Chief Minister must command the confidence of the State Legislative Assembly.

### C. Urban government

City voters → Ward Councillor / Corporator → Municipal Council or Corporation → Mayor / Chairperson and municipal administration.

Large cities, smaller towns and transitional urban areas use different local-body types.

### D. Rural government

Village voters → Gram Sabha → Gram Panchayat / Sarpanch → Panchayat Samiti / Block Panchayat → Zilla Parishad.

The intermediate tier may be absent in some states or areas; traditional and autonomous local arrangements also exist.

### E. Independent and oversight institutions

Supreme Court and High Courts • Election Commission of India • State Election Commissions • Comptroller and Auditor General of India • Public Service Commissions • statutory commissions and vigilance bodies.

### F. Citizen-facing delivery

Government schools and hospitals • police stations • revenue offices • municipal ward offices • Gram Panchayat offices • public-service centres and grievance authorities.

How to read this hierarchy: the arrows show a useful conceptual sequence, not an assertion that the President directly manages every department or that a municipal councillor reports to an MLA. Political representatives and administrators have separate legal roles.

## Part 2 — The geographical hierarchy: country to village and neighbourhood

India's territorial and administrative units are not all equivalent. A district is an administrative area; a Zilla Parishad is an elected rural local-government body; a city may be a municipal corporation; and a village may fall under a Gram Panchayat. Some of these boundaries overlap rather than nest perfectly.

### What is a village, Gram Panchayat, taluka and Zilla?

- Village: A settlement or officially recorded revenue village. One village does not always equal one Gram Panchayat.
- Gram Sabha: The body of people registered on the electoral rolls for the relevant village-panchayat area. It is a forum for participation and oversight under applicable law.
- Gram Panchayat: The elected local-government body. It can cover one village or a group of villages.
- Sarpanch / Pradhan: The elected village-panchayat leader, with the selection method and exact powers depending on the state.
- Taluka / Tehsil: A sub-district administrative and often revenue unit. It is not itself necessarily an elected government.
- Block: A development-administration unit. It may align with a taluka, but this is not universal.
- Panchayat Samiti: An intermediate rural local-government institution, where constituted.
- Zilla Parishad (ZP): An elected rural local-government body at district level. It does not automatically govern every urban area inside the geographical district.
- District Collector: The district's administrative/revenue authority under the applicable legal structure. This post is different from the elected Zilla Parishad leadership.

### How many villages are under a Sarpanch or Zilla Parishad?

There is no universal ratio. A Sarpanch represents the Gram Panchayat area, which may contain one village or several villages. A Zilla Parishad represents a district-level rural constituency or oversees the district's rural local-government functions through its elected body and administration; it does not mean that one ZP member personally represents every village in the district.

For a simplified illustration:
- 1 village might have 1 Gram Panchayat and 1 Sarpanch.
- Alternatively, 5 small villages might share 1 Gram Panchayat and 1 Sarpanch.
- A block might contain dozens or hundreds of villages.
- A district might contain several blocks and many Gram Panchayats.
- A Zilla Parishad has multiple elected members, not just one representative for the entire district.

These are structural examples, not fixed legal ratios.

## Part 7 — Urban democracy: ward, city, municipal council and corporation

Urban local government is a separate branch from rural Panchayati Raj.

### Urban bodies and their differences

### How a large city is managed

Consider Mumbai as an example.

- Citizens elect ward-level corporators and representatives to legislative constituencies.
- The Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai (MCGM/BMC) is the city's municipal local-government body.
- The Mayor provides municipal political leadership under Maharashtra's applicable municipal law.
- The Municipal Commissioner leads the municipal administration.
- The Maharashtra Urban Development Department has state-level policy and oversight functions.
- The MLA represents an Assembly constituency, which is not the same thing as a municipal ward.
- The MP represents a parliamentary constituency, which is also different from a municipal ward.
- The District Collector, police and state departments have their own distinct functions and jurisdictions.

A city may therefore have several MPs, many MLAs, numerous corporators and one Municipal Commissioner. Their areas overlap, but their powers are not identical.

## Part 9 — How departments work below the state and district level

To understand who actually carries out government work, distinguish the political department from its field administration.

### Example A: Revenue and land records

Revenue Minister

Revenue Department Secretary / Principal Secretary

Divisional Commissioner

District Collector

Sub-Divisional Officer / SDM

Tehsildar → Naib Tehsildar → Revenue Inspector / Circle Officer → Talathi / Patwari and other field staff

Names and reporting structures vary by state. The hierarchy above is illustrative.

Typical duties: land records, mutation, revenue collection, certificates and specified administrative proceedings. The competent authority and appeal route depend on the particular matter.

### Example B: Police administration

State Home Department / Home Minister

Director General of Police (DGP), or relevant Commissionerate leadership

Additional DGP / Inspector General / Deputy Inspector General, as applicable

Superintendent of Police (SP) in district systems / Police Commissioner in commissionerate systems

Additional SP / Deputy Commissioner of Police (DCP) / Assistant Commissioner of Police (ACP), depending on structure

Police station: Inspector / Station House Officer (SHO) or designated officer → Sub-Inspectors → other officers and personnel

The exact chain depends on whether the area uses a district-SP model or a police commissionerate. Police officers investigate and enforce the law within their authority; they do not decide guilt, which is a judicial function.

### Example C: Municipal public works

Urban Development Department / relevant municipal governing body

Municipal Commissioner / Chief Officer

Head of Engineering / Chief Engineer

Superintending Engineer → Executive Engineer → Assistant / Deputy Engineer → Junior Engineer / field staff

This is an illustrative engineering chain. A complaint about a broken municipal road may go to a ward office, engineering department or municipal grievance system. The corporator can advocate and follow up, but the technical inspection, funding approval, tender and execution must follow the authority assigned under law.

## Part 12 — The simplest way to understand power in India

Think of every position as having four distinct dimensions:

1\. Representation

Who elected the person, which constituency or institution they represent, and the number of electors or residents in that area.

2\. Legal authority

What laws, constitutional articles, rules or delegated powers permit the office to do.

3\. Budget and implementation

Who proposes expenditure, who approves it, who issues the sanction, who executes the work and who audits it.

4\. Accountability

Who can question, audit, investigate, review, hear an appeal or remove the officeholder under the applicable rules.

For example, an MLA may represent hundreds of thousands of electors, but a municipal engineer may have the delegated authority to inspect and execute a road repair. The MLA's political influence and the engineer's operational authority are different kinds of power.

Likewise, a Zilla Parishad President is an elected local-government leader, while the Zilla Parishad CEO is an appointed administrator. Both matter, but their roles are different.
